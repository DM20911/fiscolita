## 

The OECD's automatic exchanges of information are essential mechanisms to
improve tax transparency and combat tax evasion at the global level. Chile
has the collaboration of more than 140 countries, covering 96% of the Global
GDP, with which it is possible to know most taxpayers' operations abroad.

Source: OECD, (2024). Figure: Countries that have signed up to the Convention
on Mutual Assistance in Tax Matters (MAAC).

This information makes it possible to verify compliance by taxpayers and feed
the sources of information to detect possible non-compliance.

An example of automatic information exchange is the Common Reporting Standard
(CRS), developed in 2014, which requires financial institutions to collect
information on non-resident accounts and report it to their tax authorities.
These authorities then automatically exchange this information with other
countries, including details about account balances, revenue generated, and
the identity of account holders.

In addition to the CRS, the OECD has developed the Standard Transmission Format
(STF), which is the standard format for the international exchange of tax
information. The STF allows information to be structured and transmitted efficiently
and securely between tax authorities, ensuring consistency and data protection.
This format includes detailed information about the account holder, account
details, revenue generated, payer information, and country of residence.

Another key component is the Country-by-Country Report (CBC), part of Action 13 of the BEPS
Project. This requirement obliges multinationals to report their economic and tax activities in
each country where they operate. The information collected is automatically exchanged between tax
authorities, allowing for better assessment of the risks of base erosion and profit shifting.

In short, these OECD mechanisms not only promote tax transparency, but also
strengthen international cooperation and the capacity of countries to combat
tax evasion effectively.

### 

### Standard for Automatic Exchange of Information on Financial Accounts.

The Common Reporting Standard (*Common Reporting Standard*), is
an international framework designed to facilitate the automatic exchange
of financial information between different jurisdictions to combat tax
evasion and improve tax transparency globally.

The standard was developed by the Organization for Economic Co-operation and
Development (OECD) in collaboration with the Financial Action Task Force (FATF).
It was agreed by more than 100 countries and jurisdictions in response to
the need to address tax evasion globally.

Under the CRS standard, financial institutions in one country collect information
about their customers, including tax residents of other countries, and transmit
it to the national tax authority. The tax authority then automatically exchanges
this information with the tax authorities of other countries with which it
has information exchange agreements in place.

The information exchanged includes details about Financial Accounts, balances,
interest, dividends, and other financial income. This provides the tax authorities
with information that, when analyzed, gives indications of the possible existence
of tax evasion, as well as the concealment of financial assets abroad.

Considering the latest available information, corresponding to the 2022 business
year, through the exchange of information, **153,314 financial accounts** of
Chileans outside the country were detected, with a current account balance **of
$28 billion Chilean pesos** (MM **US$ 32,579**) and payments
of **$12.8 billion Chilean pesos** (MM **US$14,884**),
both amounts show an upward trend considering the amounts reported in previous
years.

### Standard format for transmission in terms of direct taxes.

The Standard Transmission Format (STF) is a technical format approved by the
OECD, designed to standardize the international exchange of tax information,
specifically associated with direct taxes. This format is part of the recommendations
of the OECD's SEIT (Standards for Exchange of Information in Tax Matters),
which establishes clear guidelines for the structure and content of information
exchanged between countries.

This standardized approach not only optimizes the efficiency of tax information
exchange but also strengthens international cooperation in the fight against
tax evasion. Currently, information has been received through the STF from
15 countries around the world, which contributes to a more transparent and
equitable global tax system.

After reviewing the available information corresponding to the 2020-2022 business
years, information was received on a total of 26,534 remittances in the 3
periods, with a cumulative amount of 435 billion Chilean pesos. The largest
concentration of these amounts is in Argentina, which in the 2021 business
year alone exchanged remittances received by residents or domiciled in Chile
amounting to 267,913 million CLP.   
  
Of the remittance declarations received for the 2020-2022 business years,
77% of the beneficiaries are natural persons, while 23% of them correspond
to legal entities.
