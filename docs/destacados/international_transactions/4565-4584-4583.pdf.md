I. Some Concepts of Fiscal Education
a) Taxes and Their Role in Society
Taxes are the monetary contributions that citizens
are legally obligated to pay, and they are the
primary source of income for the State, used to
fund public expenditures on goods and services for
the benefit of the population.
They play a central role in the development of a
country and the competitiveness of its businesses.
The importance of tax regulation in
macroeconomic
performance
has
been
demonstrated in specialised literature1, which
concludes that a modern tax system with clear
principles and rules promotes the creation of an
attractive
environment
for
businesses,
entrepreneurship,
and
the
creation of
opportunities.

b) The Ability-to-Pay Theory
Indicates that each citizen will pay taxes in
proportion to their economic power, based on
three parameters or indicators: wealth, income,
and expenditure (consumption).
In general terms, this theory establishes that those
who have more, earn more, and consume more,
should pay more taxes. With this proportional
contribution, the burden of public expenses is
distributed with greater social and fiscal justice.
c) International Taxation
Arises from transactions between different countries or
jurisdictions, whether through the trade of goods and/or
services, cross-border investments, or work carried out by
people outside the country in which they reside, among others.
In this context, the right of different countries to collect taxes
is established according to the following principles:
✓

Source principle: This consists of levying taxes on
transactions in the country where the income
originated, or the service was provided. This principle is
also known as the territoriality principle.

✓

Residence principle: This is based on levying taxes on
transactions in the country or jurisdiction where a
person resides2 (regardless of their nationality), without
considering the origin of the source or the income
earned.

In the case of Chile, all domiciled or resident individuals will pay
taxes on income from any origin, thereby applying the
residence principle to worldwide income. In the case of
individuals without domicile or residence in Chile, they will only
pay taxes on their Chilean income sources.
2. Legal definition of resident: Art. 8 No. 8 of the Tax Code and Art. 59 of the Civil Code.

II.

Strategy for Tax Compliance in International Transactions

Our objective as the Internal Revenue Service (Servicio de Impuesto Internos - SII) is for tax compliance
to be seen by all citizens as an essential element in building a fairer and more equitable society. Those
who decide to evade or avoid3 paying their taxes are, in practice, depriving the State of necessary
resources for all the inhabitants of the country.
According to data from the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD), tax
avoidance and evasion resulting from international tax planning have reduced global business tax
collections by an estimated USD 100 to 240 billion. In this regard, it is imperative to convey to taxpayers
that paying their taxes is a constitutional obligation for all, regardless of where in the world their assets,
goods, and transactions are located. No individual or enterprise is above the law.
The SII has designed a strategy to ensure compliance with international tax obligations. This strategy is
aligned with international practices, considering the risks and parameters present in the plan to combat
base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS)4, which provides the tools to ensure that profits are taxed where
economic activities are carried out and where value is created.
This action is developed considering a proportional response, appropriate to the risk and individual
behaviour, which guarantees equity and trust in the tax system. We focus on helping taxpayers
understand their tax obligations, facilitating their compliance, and responding firmly to those who
attempt to deceive the system. Our strategy aims to guarantee tax compliance in international
transactions and to ensure that there is no room for evasion or avoidance regarding such cross-border
transactions.

V.

Supervision based on risk analysis models

The tax compliance of taxpayers is the core function of the SII. This requires, on the one hand, a
comprehensive understanding of taxpayers or segments thereof, their activities or business models, and
the levels of tax compliance applicable to them. On the other hand, it demands high levels of efficiency
and proportionality in the actions undertaken by the institution for these purposes.
To manage this essential function, including incentivising voluntary tax compliance and systematising a
comprehensive view of taxpayers' tax behaviour or segments thereof, appropriately understanding noncompliance risks and the context in which they arise, determining the design and implementation of
programmes with corresponding treatment actions, and achieving institutional objectives, over the last
decade, the SII implemented a Tax Compliance Management Model based on risk analysis8.
The automation of analysis corresponding to each defined risk is conducted using information provided
by taxpayers and third parties, State agencies, and foreign tax administrations, as well as data obtained
from international organisations and public sources. This encompasses strategies for risk identification
and strategies to mitigate those risks.

VI.

Tax Intermediaries

The SII recognises the important role that
intermediaries10 play in collaborating with
taxpayers to comply with their tax obligations.
Their assistance allows for reducing their
administrative burden and guaranteeing the
payment of the correct tax amount within the
corresponding legal periods. This is particularly
relevant when taxpayers' tax matters involve
international transactions.
While we acknowledge that the majority of
intermediaries provide services in accordance
with current regulations and promote legal
behaviour, there is a small group that, together
with some financial institutions, facilitate tax
avoidance and evasion. As the Internal Revenue
Service, we will direct all our audit efforts against
such behaviours, whether it is a planning scheme
facilitated to various taxpayers or one “tailored”
for a particular taxpayer. They will be audited,
and all powers will be used to enforce
compliance with the law and sanction such
infractions.

As part of our strategy, we work permanently
with intermediaries to maximise their role in
promoting tax compliance through collaborative
efforts and by providing digital services that
facilitate their labour. We work on improving and
expanding the assistance channels available to
intermediaries and their clients.

Intermediaries who design, promote, or
commercialise tax schemes that facilitate tax
evasion will be sanctioned with fines of up to
100% of the evaded taxes and risk imprisonment
penalties9.
We will not only take necessary actions to ensure
severe sanctions for those who encourage noncompliance but also aim for their recognition by
society as promoters of dishonest behaviours that
affect the citizenry. Therefore, in the event that
taxpayers and intermediaries are found guilty by
the justice system, we will disclose their identities
and the rationale behind their sanctions.
9. Articles 100 and 100 bis of the Tax Code.
10. Intermediaries encompass all providers of tax advisory services, including attorneys, accountants, banks, and consultancy
enterprises.

VII.

Sanctions and Responsible Parties in Case of Tax Non-Compliance

There is a common misconception that the
consequences of non-compliance, such as fines
and/or sanctions, only affect the enterprise and
can be considered as just another expense to be
paid if audited. Many people are unaware that
those responsible for an enterprise's noncompliance are those who manage it, and the
punishments established in such cases can be
severe. When it comes to non-compliance, the
law is clear11:
• Monetary sanctions are imposed on the
taxpayer and any other legally obligated
individuals. In the case of legal persons,
responsibility extends to the general
manager, administrator, or those acting in
their capacity, as well as their partners.
• Corporal
sanctions
and
distraints
(imprisonment) will be applied to individuals
who should have complied with the tax
obligation. In the case of legal persons, they
apply to the managers, administrators, or
those acting in their capacity, as well as the
partners.

In this context, the responsibility and
consequences of non-compliance rest on the
natural persons who manage the legal persons.
There are no dishonest or deceitful enterprises
seeking to exploit or gain advantages; it is the
individuals managing them who engage in such
practices.
Planning schemes aimed solely at reducing tax
payments are ineffective. The majority of disputes
are settled administratively, and if they escalate to
the courts, taxpayers lose 7 out of 10 times against
the SII12.
We expect that by transparently outlining the
consequences associated with the use of elusive
tax planning, coupled with the increased likelihood
of being audited and identified by the SII, taxpayers
will feel compelled to steer clear of such
behaviours. As the Internal Revenue Service, we
are committed to delivering a proportional
response, appropriate to the risk and behaviour, to
ensure equity and trust in the tax system.

VIII. Conclusions
The SII has implemented international standards regarding transparency and BEPS risk management,
utilising risk analysis-based models, and strengthened its relationships with foreign tax administrations.
More countries are entering into agreements enabling the exchange of information.
The information received from third parties, other tax administrations, and State agencies, as well as data
obtained from international organisations and public sources, makes it challenging for taxpayers to hide
their wealth abroad without the SII becoming aware of their transactions.
International collaboration and participation in multilateral teams with foreign tax administrations and
expert teams from international organisations have enabled the identification of emerging risks and the
implementation of strategies to address them in alignment with international standards. This
collaboration, coupled with information exchanges, enables the SII to proactively conduct its processes,
reducing the time between the implementation of a planning and its detection, even before noncompliance occurs.

Our strategy, while facilitating voluntary tax compliance, also monitors and sanctions taxpayers
attempting to evade or reduce their taxes, especially focusing on non-compliance involving entities and
transactions with elusive characteristics.
We urge taxpayers to comply with their tax obligations voluntarily, regularise their situation if they have
undisclosed wealth abroad, provide the SII with comprehensive and irrefutable information regarding
foreign investments and incomes, and include all their incomes in their tax declarations.
We encourage citizens to report any irregular transactions they become
aware of through our web page.

www.sii.cl / Contact / Report Evasion.
