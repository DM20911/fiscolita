## 

Chile has adopted a tax credit method for relieving double taxation.  
  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 41 A of the LIR, taxpayers or
entities domiciled, resident, incorporated or established in Chile, which
obtain income that has borne taxes abroad, will be governed by the rules of
this article for the purposes of using as a credit the taxes paid on such
income:

1. **Income subject to FTC**

1. Dividends and withdrawals of profits;
2. Income from the use of intangibles, such as trademarks, patents and formulas;
3. Income from the provision of professional or technical services, or qualified
   export services;
4. Income from salaries or fees;
5. Income from a permanent establishment (PE) located abroad;
6. Passive income resulting from the application of the provisions of Article
   41G of the LIR;
7. Income from countries with which Chile has signed an agreement to avoid double
   taxation, which is in force and in which it has committed to grant a credit
   for the income tax or taxes paid in the other contracting country.

2. **Taxes paid abroad that can be used as a credit**

1. Direct credit for withholding tax;
2. Indirect credit for corporate tax;
3. Indirect credit in respect of subsidiary amounts;
4. Additional Tax Credit

3. **Credit Determination**

At the end of each fiscal year, taxpayers will determine a Taxable Income
and a Net Income for the purposes of using the credit.   
  
**Taxable Income** will be made up of the Net Income plus
all taxes that can be used as credits, contemplating the corresponding cap.   
**Net Income** will correspond to the consolidated result
of net income received or loss in relation to income that bore taxes abroad
and that constitutes income subject to taxes in Chile, deducting the proportion
of expenses incurred to produce it.   
**Total Available Credit** will correspond to taxes borne
abroad, which may not exceed the lesser amount between the Individual cap
and the Global cap.

* **The Individual Cap** will correspond to the lesser amount
  between the tax effectively paid abroad and 35% on the gross income of each
  type of income taxed abroad. When gross income cannot be credited, the credit
  may not exceed 35% of an amount such that, when subtracting said 35%, the
  result is equivalent to the net income received.
* **The Global Cap** will correspond to 35% of the amount resulting
  from being added to the Net Income each year, the lesser amount between the
  taxes borne abroad and the individual cap already described.

Taxpayers who declare passive income, in accordance with Article 41G of the
LIR, will calculate the individual and global capped for the use of the credits
in the year in which such income is accrued. At the time of receiving the
income, the taxpayer must recalculate the capped. The credit used in the year
in which the passive income of article 41G was accrued must be deducted from
the recalculated credit.

4. **Credit allocations**

The annual credit for taxes paid abroad duly determined may be deducted from
the following taxes:

* Corporative Tax.
* Personal Income Tax.
* Payroll Tax.

5. **Other provisions**

When taxpayers make investments, they must report them to the Tax Administration
of Chile (SII), through the instructions given by said entity for the preparation
of the Affidavit 1929 on Operations Abroad.   
  
For the accreditation of taxes paid, withheld or owed abroad, the taxpayer
must provide proof of payment or declaration of taxes abroad, or an official
certificate issued by the competent authority of the foreign country. When
this background information is not available, the taxpayer may prove it by
any means of legal proof.
